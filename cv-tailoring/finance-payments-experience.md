## 1. EY — Procurement Function Improvement (major Ukrainian bank, recent — last year)

- Recent project, performed last year, for one of the major Ukrainian banks.
- Focus: procurement implementation and procurement function improvement.

**Audit scope:**
- Audited procurement processes and procurement/budget control processes.
- Investigated and advised on better controls for procurement of licenses, software, and license fees — these categories represented more than 70% of the bank's total operational budget expenditures.

**Digital Asset Management (DAM) system design:**
- Advised and prepared the design for a digital asset management system covering the full lifecycle: request → justification → application/allocation of the asset (additional steps may need to be added later).
- Core control principle: any license request must be justified and tied back to the specific project or business activity it serves, in order to properly allocate cost.
- Requests must be processed through tender procedures — to avoid duplicate purchases and to capture bulk discounts available through license hubs/resellers.
- System had to track actual end users of each license: real named users, their divisions, and the business products the licenses were allocated to.
- System had to store/track issues, reclamations, and similar records related to the digital assets.
- System had to consolidate vendor communication in a single place — previously this was spread across individual business owners, making it impossible to track negotiations or evaluate whether end consumers (on the bank side) were engaging with vendors efficiently.

**Procurement structure & category management (higher-level, described first in narrative):**
- Suggested a redesigned procurement structure.
- Described in detail the approach for category management as it relates to procurement and budgeting.
- Main emphasis: category management — and specifically the processes around it (process improvement framing throughout: "we improved this process, we improved that process").

**Project outcome:**
- Sponsor was the advisory board of the bank.
- The engagement did not continue into the implementation phase — the client was a big, state-owned Ukrainian bank, and implementation did not proceed (organizational/state-ownership factors).

## 3. Kernel — ERP Implementation (Cost Control for Field Operations)

- Implementation of ERP for Kernel (large Ukrainian agribusiness).
- Core deliverable: calculated the total cost of field operations by connecting several production (operational) systems, applying different coefficients, and calculating the actual cost of the operation payable to the third-party supplier who performed the machinery work (field operation machinery services).

**Business context:**
- Served as an independent control system for Kernel to check and confirm invoices sent by suppliers and subcontractors.
- Kernel contracted various subcontractors who supplied their own machinery — tractors, conveyors, truck drivers, etc.
- A large part of the machinery fleet was rented by Kernel, creating a complex web of interpayments and interconnections between Kernel, subcontractors, and fleet owners.
- This complexity could only be controlled by calculating the actual amount of work performed, derived from operational (production) systems data — not from financial records alone.

**Andrii's contribution:** designed the cross-system calculation logic (connecting operational systems + coefficients) that produced the independently verified cost figure used to check/confirm supplier and subcontractor invoices.
